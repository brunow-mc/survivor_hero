**`entities/stage_parts/room_door.tscn` + `scripts/room_door.gd` (`class_name RoomDoor`)** — `Area2D` (layer none, **mask = layer 2 only**, `monitorable` off) + `CollisionShape2D` (`RectangleShape2D`, **`resource_local_to_scene = true`** so resizing one instance doesn't resize all) + a child **`Marker2D` named `ArrivalPoint`**. It listens to `body_entered`, so what triggers it is the player's **physics body** — the feet collider — never the `Hurtbox` (an `Area2D`, which would need `area_entered`). No sprite: the door's look comes from each room's tiles. Same script in every `GameplayState`.
- **Pairing: `@export var partner: RoomDoor`**, a node export picked in the Inspector; `class_name` makes the field accept only room doors. Each door points to its partner **independently** (A→B and B→A are two settings) — that is what makes one-way trivial: **an empty `partner` means "arrival only"**; the door still receives the player but touching it goes nowhere. Empty is a declaration, same discipline as `enemy_spawn_excluded_layers`. Auto-pairing was rejected: it would need `@tool`, whose editor traps are in *`@tool` scenes*, to save one click. Renaming or reparenting doors keeps the link (the editor rewrites `NodePath`s — confirmed in practice when the safe house doors were renamed); deleting the partner breaks it.
- **Naming convention: `DoorNNA` ↔ `DoorNNB`** — the number names the **link**, the letter the end (`Door01A` ↔ `Door01B`, `Door02A` ↔ `Door02B`). Reading a node name tells you its partner, and it scales without running out of letters (`DoorA`/`DoorB`/`DoorC` did not say who pairs with whom). A one-way link keeps the same pair naming; only the arrival end's `partner` stays empty.
- **Why the player does not bounce straight back: they arrive OUTSIDE the trigger.** `body_entered` fires only on an outside→inside transition, so arriving at an `ArrivalPoint` placed a step into the room means going back requires walking to the door again — a fresh, legitimate entry (the *A Link to the Past* solution). The alternative "re-arm only after the player leaves the area" was rejected: a player who arrives outside never leaves the area, so the door would ignore their first real attempt. A cooldown was rejected for depending on time. The `ArrivalPoint` marks the **feet** (the player scene's origin), and being a child of the door it moves with it. Moving it on an instance needs **Editable Children** on that instance.
- **Misplacement is warned at init**, per door: partner pointing at itself; missing `ArrivalPoint`; `ArrivalPoint` inside the door's own area **or closer than `ARRIVAL_CLEARANCE`** (a `const` at the top of the script, an approximate margin for the feet collider's extent). Before `HoldGlobal`, a misplaced point produced **one bounce** (arriving inside door B sent the player straight back to door A's arrival point); the hold now absorbs that — see the crossing bullet below.
- **The crossing is not instant: hold → fade out → move in the dark → fade in → release**, through `HoldGlobal` (`TRANSITION` set) and `FadeGlobal`. Durations are the constants `HOLD_BEFORE_MOVE` (fade-out) and `HOLD_AFTER_MOVE` (fade-in) at the top of `room_door.gd` — the door owns them, the fade only executes. The player is moved while the screen is fully black, so no jump is ever visible, and regains control only after the fade-in completes. The fade-out also takes the move out of the physics callback the touch arrived in. **The door ignores the player while their input is held** — whoever cannot walk does not enter doors. That blocks chaining doors, and it neutralises a misplaced `ArrivalPoint`: arriving inside the destination door's area, the touch happens during the hold and is ignored; on release the player is already inside (no new entry) and must step out and back in. The init warning still matters — the door does not break, but the player appears on top of it. Moving the player within a scene dangles nothing: the enemies' cached `BodyCenter` node and `SpawnManagerGlobal.player` still point at the same node. **The camera needs no snap code**: it copies the player's body position every physics frame and `position_smoothing_enabled` is off (`position_smoothing_speed` being set in `camera.tscn` means nothing while it is off) — turning smoothing on would make it slide across the map on every room change.
- **Room layout rule for COMBAT stages:** separate rooms by more than the spawn band's reach (half-viewport + `spawn_margin_max_*`), or enemies spawn in the neighbouring room. And expect **the horde to follow** the player through a door: everyone left behind exceeds the teleport distance and gets recycled into the new room.

**`entities/stage_parts/stage_door.tscn` + `scripts/stage_door.gd` (`class_name StageDoor`)** — same physical setup as the room door (`Area2D`, layer none, mask layer 2, `monitorable` off, local-to-scene `RectangleShape2D`) but **no `ArrivalPoint`**: whoever arrives in a new stage appears at its `PlayerSpawner`. One-way by nature. Name instances by destination (`ToStage01`, `ToSafeHouse`).
- **Destination is `@export_file("*.tscn") var destination: String`, never a `PackedScene`.** A `PackedScene` export makes the scene a *dependency*, so opening the safe house would load every stage it has a door to (scaling with the sum of all stages' unique assets, paid on every return to the safe house), and safe house ↔ stage pointing at each other would be a **cyclic dependency Godot cannot load**. A string loads nothing until `change_scene_to_file()` on touch.
- **It is still addressed by uid — this is NOT an exception to the `uid://` rule.** The `.tscn` stores `destination = "uid://…"` (verified in both stages); the Inspector merely *displays* the resolved `res://` path so a human can tell which file it is. Moving or renaming a stage keeps every door working.
- The door only detects the player and calls `GameStateGlobal.go_to_scene(destination, FADE_OUT_TIME, FADE_IN_TIME)` (its own constants). Init warnings: empty destination; destination that no longer exists (`ResourceLoader.exists`). Ignores the player while their input is held.
